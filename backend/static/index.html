Welcome to Medical Concierge

When several doctors each see only part of the picture, things get missed — a duplicate prescription, an interaction nobody caught, a supplement no one was told about. This app is the one place that holds all of it, so you can see the whole picture and bring it to your next appointment.

How it works

  1. Add your documents. Visit notes, pharmacy printouts, medication charts, photos of pill and supplement bottles, even handwritten notes. PDFs and phone photos both work — you don't need to sort or label them.
  2. Your medication list builds itself. Medicines and supplements are read out of each document and merged into one list. The same drug seen in three documents becomes one entry, not three.
  3. Everything is checked against everything else. Interactions, duplicate prescriptions from different doctors, and nutrients your medicines may deplete — each with a plain-language explanation.
  4. Take it to your doctor. One button produces a clean PDF summary with the warnings up front, ready to print or hand over.

Where the information comes from

Your own documents Read by Claude's vision AI. Every entry carries a confidence score, and anything it wasn't sure about is flagged “check this” with the exact uncertainty spelled out — for example “dose digit unclear, could be 10 or 40.”
RxNorm — U.S. National Library of Medicine The official drug vocabulary. It's what turns “Tylenol,” “APAP,” and a scrawled abbreviation into one correctly identified ingredient, so brand and generic names of the same drug never show up as two different medicines.
A built-in interaction reference list Well-documented drug–drug, drug–supplement, vitamin/mineral, duplicate-therapy, and nutrient-depletion facts — the kind printed on a pharmacy handout.
This is a starter screen, not a complete interaction check. It catches well-known problems worth asking about; it does not replace the full screening your pharmacist can run.

Getting the best results

  • Add everything you have. The app can only reason about what it's been shown — more documents means fewer blind spots.
  • Review the “check this” items first. Those are the readings the app is least sure of, and a quick correction there makes every warning downstream more reliable.
  • Keep the list honest. Fix doses and press Stop on anything you no longer take. Your edits always win — a new document can never silently overwrite what you've confirmed.
  • Set a baseline after each visit. Then Compare to baseline shows exactly what changed since — ideal to review before the next appointment.
  • Bring the PDF with you. It's designed for a clinician skimming it in a few minutes, with the warnings first.

Your privacy

Your records live in a single file on this computer — no account, no cloud, no medical database holding your history. Only two things ever leave this machine: document images go to Anthropic's API to be read, and drug names only go to RxNorm to be identified. Start over saves a complete PDF archive before erasing anything.

One important thing

This is not a medical device and nothing here is medical advice. It organizes your information and flags things worth asking about — never start, stop, or change a medicine based on what you see here. Bring it to your doctor or pharmacist and let them decide.

Expert panel review

A panel of subject-matter experts — pharmacists, organ-system specialists, a deprescribing voice, a skeptic who argues most warnings are noise, and someone speaking from lived experience — reviews your list independently, argues with each other, and reports what they couldn't agree on. This is separate from the warnings above, which come from a fixed reference table rather than from reasoning.
